Businesses lose real money every month because their records don't match the government's
1

A shop buys stock

Sharma Traders, a Delhi wholesaler, buys goods worth ₹1,00,000 and pays ₹18,000 GST on top to its supplier. Its accountant records the bill in the books.

2

That GST can come back

When the shop later sells goods, it can subtract the ₹18,000 it already paid from the GST it owes. This credit is called input tax credit (ITC), and it works like cash in hand.

3

Only if every record agrees

The supplier must report the same sale on the GST portal, the bill must charge the right rate, and the shop must pay within 180 days. Books, bank and portal must all tell one story.

4

One mismatch, money gone

If even one detail differs, the credit is denied, the business pays interest, or a tax notice arrives months later. Often it surfaces only when it is too late to fix.
